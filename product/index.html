Product

A base workbench, licensed modules, your hardware.

Sold the way test instruments are sold: one install, every module present, each lit by a licence key. The hardware carries no margin.

The workbench

Every unit ships with it.

Intake of a raw recording with verification; a first look at what the recording holds; grouping of the energy in it into signals, each linked to the radio that sent it where the evidence allows; and an assistant that works the hypotheses, controls and dead ends so the engineer can spend their attention on the call.

Modules

RF Analysis

The main line. Drop in a recording and get every signal in it found, grouped to the radio that sent it and described in the words an RF engineer uses. Take an unknown apart step by step against a reference library of public standards.

RF Baseline and Change

Learn the normal radio picture of a place, follow the signals that change — bandwidth, centre, timing — and flag what deviates from the baseline, across recordings taken on different days.

Satellite Transponder Survey

Catalogue every carrier on a transponder, recognise a hub network by its timing, census the terminals active in each frame — a snapshot of who is on the air, with the network timing stated.

Drone-Link Class

Identify the class of a drone's control or video link from its physical footprint in a recording — one radio or several, and what changed. Never content.

Where the model sits

An assistant drives the exhaustive part of every analysis. It is designed to run where your network allows: with a hosted model, with a model you already operate on your premises, or entirely on the box for air-gapped networks. The RF software underneath is the same in all three, and every number comes from signal-processing code — never from the model's say-so.
